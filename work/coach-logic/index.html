The operating system behind a founder-led sports technology company.

Coach Logic brought me in to run a content engine. Six months later I was building the company's operating system in Notion: dashboards, a custom CRM, meetings that turn into tasks, research that ranks who to sell to next, and a client portal.

Content exposed the gap underneath.

The brief was to turn the founders' day-to-day work, their customer calls, trips and events, into content, with AI taking out the manual effort.

Running that engine showed the systems underneath weren't there. Sales notes sat as free text at the bottom of deal pages, so nobody could see every open action in one place. The same four people were spelled four different ways across seven databases, so filtering by owner didn't work. Once a customer signed, there was nowhere to track onboarding or growth. A first attempt at dashboards had put charts on top of data that was a third untagged. It looked finished and told you nothing.

Sales and product were both strong. Nothing connected them, and nobody owned whether a signed customer's coaches actually used the product. So the remit grew from content into operations.

How I built it.

The approach matters as much as the parts. It is why the system still makes sense to people who didn't build it.

  • Audit before building

    Every database was read for how full it actually was before anything was designed on top of it. A field existing is not data existing.

  • Tear down and rebuild

    The first pass was deliberately taken apart and rebuilt one database at a time, with the founders walking through each one before moving on.

  • Governed schemas

    Core databases have a closed, documented set of fields. Nothing gets added without a decision.

  • One living build record

    A single running document holds every schema, decision and platform limit, so someone other than me can maintain it.

  • Built around how people work

    Tasks reach founders without a flood of notifications. A captured date sits apart from a due date, so migrated tasks didn't all show as overdue on day one.

  • A person approves every write

    Claude drafts, structures and files. Someone on the team approves, and nothing is ever published automatically.

What I built.

The layouts, fields and views are real. Client writing is blurred out.

Live

Operations dashboards

One dashboard per person, built around their job rather than a template. Each opens on their own tasks, then the content waiting for their review, then the customers who are growing.

  • Commercial lead: pipeline board, tasks, expanding accounts
  • Product lead: tasks, content review calendar, expanding accounts
  • A weekly "this week's focus" note on each profile
Live

A CRM built for how they sell

Built in Notion rather than bought, and shaped around a sports technology company selling to governing bodies.

  • A governed pipeline: stage, deal type, owner, organisation type, lead source, coach base
  • A post-sale layer: onboarding, usage, and whether an account is landed, expanding or mature
  • Hierarchical accounts: a national body, the regions beneath it, the clubs beneath those
  • A referral network, with who introduced whom and whether consent to quote is on file
Live

Meetings that turn into tasks

Meetings are captured with Notion's AI Meeting Notes, set up so every summary comes out in the same structure: decisions, actions, owners, deal context. Custom agents then file the notes against the right deal and turn agreed actions into owned tasks on each person's dashboard.

Every deal links both ways to its conversations and its next steps, so opening a deal shows its whole history.

Live

Research that ranks who to call

Desk research turned into a ranked target list. An events database screens every coaching event in a customer's regions for fit. A club roster records whether each club employs a head of coaching and what tools they use.

A transparent formula estimates each club's coaching workforce from public data, then ranks every club nationally and within its region. The assumptions sit in the formula, so they can be changed and re-run.

Live

The content engine

Call transcripts land in an inbox, rated for content potential. Claude skills turn a transcript or clip into drafts in a named founder's voice, governed by a written voice and rules file, then file them to the calendar for review.

Founder calendars are scanned for trips and events, and each gets a brief so content is planned before the event, not scrambled after.

Phase 1 built

A client portal

A secured space where each customer gets their own portal page: their tasks, session notes and resources, a programme roadmap with a progress bar, and invoices.

Each client sees only their own rows, enforced by permission rules rather than copied pages. A built-in check flags any record whose sharing doesn't match its client, so nothing reaches the wrong customer. Gated onboarding content comes next.

Live, early

Project tracking

A company-wide Live Projects board, one row per project and per growing customer account, with board and timeline views. Linking projects to tasks is next.

Live

The AI layer

Inside Notion, custom agents handle recurring admin like meeting follow-up. Alongside it, Claude connects to Notion, Google Calendar, HubSpot and Canva to run bounded jobs: call to content, trip briefs, calendar radar, weekly profile refreshes and research. Each one checks the live database before writing and asks before acting.
